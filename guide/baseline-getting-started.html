Baseline

Getting started

Baseline decides what you should train today — from how recovered you are and what you have actually been doing. Not a plan written weeks ago. A decision made this morning.

This guide takes about five minutes to read. You only need it once.

Everything in the app stays on your own phone.
No account, no sign-up, nothing uploaded anywhere.
What it is

One session a day, and the reason for it

Most training apps hand you a calendar decided in advance: legs on Tuesday, whether or not you slept badly, played a match on Monday, or feel like you have been hit by a bus.

Baseline asks you a few questions each morning, then gives you one session — and tells you why it chose that one and what it ruled out. If you disagree, you change it, and it adapts around what you actually did.

What it needs from you

  • About a minute each morning
  • A tap when you finish a session
  • Anything else you do, logged in three taps

What it does not need

  • An account or a password
  • A gym, or any equipment at all
  • A watch or fitness tracker
  • An internet connection, after the first load

A day in the app

  1. Open it and check in. How long you slept, four sliders for how you feel, whether anything hurts, and how long you have. The sliders start where you left them, so most days are a nudge or two.
  2. Read today's session. A name, a duration, an intensity. Nothing else competes with it on the screen.
  3. Tap “Why this?” if you want the reasoning — what counted, and what it decided against.
  4. Start it. The screen becomes the workout: one exercise at a time, what you lifted last time, and plus/minus buttons instead of a keyboard.
  5. Tell it how that felt — one tap at the end. This is the single thing that most changes what you get next time.
  6. Log anything else. A match, a walk, helping a friend move house. Unplanned activity is not a missed workout.
Nothing is assumed about you. No sport, no equipment, no goal, no tracker, no injuries — every one of those starts empty, and empty is a perfectly good answer. Tick nothing at all and you still get a complete bodyweight programme.
Step 1 of 2

Put it on your Home Screen — iPhone & iPad

Baseline is a website that behaves like an app. Adding it to your Home Screen gives it its own icon, makes it open full-screen without the browser bars, and — importantly — tells iPhone to keep your data.

You must use Safari for this

Chrome and other browsers on iPhone cannot add a working app to the Home Screen. If you opened the link somewhere else, copy it into Safari first.

  1. Open the link in Safari. Tap the link you were sent. Wait for it to finish loading.
  2. Tap the Share button. It is the square with an arrow pointing up, at the bottom of the screen (or the top right on an iPad).
  3. Scroll down and tap “Add to Home Screen”. It is a fair way down the list, past the sharing options.
  4. Tap “Add” at the top right. The icon appears on your Home Screen.
  5. Open it from that icon from now on — not from Safari.
This is the icon you are looking for.
It will be named Baseline. You can rename it while adding it if you would rather call it something else.
This matters more than it looks. Left as an ordinary Safari tab, iPhone may delete the app's saved data after about a week of you not opening it. Added to the Home Screen, it is treated as a real app and kept.
Step 1 of 2

…and on Android

  1. Open the link in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three-dot menu at the top right.
  3. Tap “Install app”, or “Add to Home screen” if that is what yours says.
  4. Confirm with “Install” or “Add”.

On a laptop it works too: in Chrome or Edge there is a small install icon at the right-hand end of the address bar. On a Mac, Safari has File → Add to Dock.

Does the installed version share my data with the browser tab?

Yes, as long as the web address is identical. They are the same app. It is still worth using the icon, for the reason above.

Finishing an exercise moves you on by itself — after four seconds, counted on screen, and cancelled the moment you touch anything. Reaching for the undo chip is itself a reason not to move on.

“This hurt”

One tap. That exercise is left out of your sessions for three weeks and something covering the same movement is swapped in immediately, so you can carry on. Sharp or lasting pain is worth a word with a physio or doctor rather than only an app setting.

At the end

A summary, and one question: Too easy · About right · Too hard. It is a single tap and it is the most useful thing you can tell the app — after three sessions rated the same way, it starts adjusting how much work it gives you.

Stop half way through and nothing is lost. The exit button always asks, and Pause keeps everything exactly as it is — Today offers the session back when you return.
The rest of the app

Three more sections

Train

Opens with this week's targets and where you are in the eight-week progression. Underneath: your workouts, the exercise library filtered to your equipment, warm-ups, and every session you have finished.

Progression is earned, not scheduled. Miss a fortnight and the plan waits for you rather than marching on to a peak week you have not worked for.

Progress

Opens with how your week actually went. Then, one tap each: whether individual lifts are moving, fitness test results, body measurements, and patterns the app has noticed in your own data — always described as a correlation, never a cause.

Logging anything else

Two ways, and they ask different questions because they know different things.

Start activity — pick what you are doing from a searchable list and the clock runs. Time and strain both count from the moment you press start until you press stop, with laps if you want them. How hard it was is asked at the end, because before you start it is a guess.

Add activity — for something already finished. It asks when it started, when it ended, and what strain it came to. The length works itself out from the two times, and the strain is filled in with the app's own estimate which you can type over if your watch gave you a number.

Forgot to log Saturday? Move back a week with the arrow on the calendar, tap the day, and add it there. The card follows the day you selected, so Add activity means that day — and Back to today returns you.

Important

Your data, and the one thing to remember

Everything you enter lives in your browser, on your phone. There is no account, no server and nothing to sign into — and no way for anyone else, including me, to see any of it. If two people open the same link they each get an empty app, not each other's data.

If you import a wearable export

You export your own data from WHOOP, Garmin, Oura or similar and pick the files yourself. Baseline is not connected to any of those services — no account, no password, no integration. It reads the file you chose, in your browser.

What it keepsThe daily figures: recovery, HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, strain, and your workouts
What it never keepsThe file itself, and the free-text notes in a journal export
Where it goesYour browser's storage, on that device
What is uploadedNothing
How to remove itSettings → Data → Delete imported data, which tells you exactly what it removed

Settings → Data → Erase all data removes everything on the device instead, and starts the app over. One thing worth knowing: Baseline has no passcode of its own, so anyone who can unlock your phone can open it.

That also means a backup is the only copy that exists. If you clear your browser data, lose the phone, or delete the app, it is gone. There is nowhere to restore it from.

So: back it up

Settings → Data → Backup saves one file with everything in it. Restore reads it back, including onto a new phone. It takes five seconds. Worth doing every few weeks and before changing phones.

If something looks wrong

  1. Close the app completely and reopen it. Not minimise — close it, then open it again. Twice, if a new version has just been released.
  2. Check the version. It is at the bottom of the Settings screen.
  3. Send me the diagnostics. Settings → About → Copy diagnostics puts device, browser and storage details on your clipboard, plus counts of how much you have logged. It never includes anything you typed. Paste it into a message along with what you were doing.

Common things

“It says storage is not working.” Add it to your Home Screen (page 3) and open it from there. Private browsing windows also block storage.

“My data disappeared.” Almost always because it was used as a browser tab on an iPhone and iOS cleared it. Home Screen plus a backup prevents this.

Baseline is not a medical device. It does not diagnose anything. Persistent pain, unusual symptoms or readings that worry you belong with a qualified healthcare professional, separately from any training decision.
